# Dark Dungeoneer — Story & World Bible

The narrative reference for Dark Dungeoneer. It turns the existing systems (darkness, the lantern, senses, reshuffling floors, the Meta-Ledger, co-op) into one fiction and names the new combat kit. Everything here is original to this project.

---

## 1. Premise

Under the hill called the Hesp lies Senwick, a city of lamps and mirrors that, three hundred years ago, refused to let one particular night end. It is still that night down there. The dead get up because death needs a tomorrow to finish in, and the hill has been quietly drinking the mornings of the valley above to keep its night going. This year the dawn bell in the village of Hespfoot rang in full darkness. You are Hespfoot's lamplighter, and you carry the one flame that came out of Senwick when the night was sealed. You go down the Stair with it. You die. You wake at the top of the Stair at the same dusk, and you go down again.

---

## 2. The World and Its History

**Hespfoot** is a village of slate roofs and sheep at the foot of the Hesp. Its chapel has a lantern that must never go out. Nobody remembers why any more, only that the lamplighter's first duty is that lantern and the street lamps come second.

**Senwick** was the city inside the hill, built by glassmakers. They sank shafts through the rock and lined them with mirrors, so sunlight came down into the mountain and was passed from mirror to mirror into every street. A council of master glaziers, the **Glass Synod**, governed it and kept careful books of how much light each district received. Below the lit streets lay the ossuaries, where every body was buried with a lamp; below those, the fungus gardens and fish ponds that fed the city; and at the bottom, the great kiln where the mirrors were poured.

The **Wickwardens** kept the lamps of the deep places lit. They tended grave-lamps, carried light into the mines, and trained themselves to work in the dark when the light failed: listening to the rock, reading warmth on stone, seeing by almost nothing.

Three hundred years ago an army came up the valley from the coast, expected at the Hesp by dawn. The Synod did not trust the gates, so they decided dawn would not come. In the great kiln they poured a mirror called the **Stillglass**, and in it they caught the city's last night and held it, the way a mirror holds a face. The army found a hill with no way in and, after a season, went home. The night is still being held.

The Wardens call this the **Held Hour**. The same night happens over and over. Water runs and fungus grows, but nothing can finish. A death cannot complete; it only starts again. After three centuries of dying without finishing, what is left of Senwick's people runs on habit alone.

Holding a night costs time, and inside the hour there is none to spare, so the Stillglass draws it from outside: the mornings of the valley. At first it was seconds a year, then minutes. This year the dawn did not come before the bell.

The army has been dust for two hundred and fifty years. The night meant to keep it out is still being kept.

## 3. The Protagonist and the Co-op Partner

### The Bearer

The player character is Hespfoot's lamplighter, called **the Bearer**. Hooded and cloaked, in the leathers of someone who climbs ladders in the rain; the steel bracers are lamplighter's guards against hot glass and hooks. They carry no sword. They carry the chapel lantern. They are not chosen and have no special blood. They had the job when the bell rang in the dark, and they are stubborn about doing a job properly. The Ledger never names them: it records "the lamplighter of Hespfoot" and the number of the descent.

### The lantern

The chapel lantern belonged to **Ysolde Crane**, a Wickwarden who walked out of Senwick on the night the Stillglass was poured. Hers is the only flame in the world that was lit before the Held Hour began and is not part of it.

That is why it is a weapon. Inside the Hesp, light is the only thing still moving forward in time. When the Bearer swings the lantern, the flame throws an edge of light, and a held thing struck by a moving thing is cut. The Wardens called this the **lantern blade** and built their lanterns with edged panes to throw it cleanly.

The flame still needs oil to burn bright, and Senwick is full of grave-lamps and mine-lamps to drain. When the oil runs low the flame shrinks to a bead, the dark takes the room back, and the blade dulls. Oil is light, life and edge at once.

The **Lantern Trims** are the lamplighter's old craft of trimming a wick to change how it burns: Amber (the plain flame), Sun-Gold (short and hot), Cryptfire (the greenish flame Wardens used in the ossuaries) and Amethyst (a mirror-hall trim, the colour light turns after too many reflections). They are a matter of taste.

### The second lantern-bearer

A flame can be lit from another flame. Anyone who lights a wick from the Bearer's lantern carries a flame that is also outside the hour, and the hour lets them in. Locally, this is a second villager who refused to let the lamplighter go down alone; online, a stranger who saw a light in the dark and lit their wick from it. Two lanterns, one flame. That gives co-op its rules:

- **Downed and revived.** When a bearer falls, their wick gutters but cannot go out while the other flame burns. The partner relights it by holding their lantern close. Leave it too long and the wick goes cold.
- **Shared stash.** The hour counts two bearers of one flame as one person, so their purse is common: the **Twin Satchel**. Trading between bearers is two people swapping pockets by lamplight.

## 4. Why Runs Repeat

**Why the Bearer comes back.** When the Bearer dies in the Hesp, the death cannot finish. The hour turns back to its beginning, which for anyone who entered from outside is dusk at the **Stair-Head**, the Wardens' lamp-niche at the top of the Stair. Senwick's dead are caught in the same turning but carry no memory through it. The Bearer does, because the flame stands outside the hour and relights them at the Stair-Head with everything they learned.

**Why the dungeon reshuffles.** The city inside the hour is a reflection in the Stillglass. Each time the hour turns back, the city is reflected again, and a reflection of a reflection is never exact. Rooms return in a different order and the Vendor sets up somewhere new, but the bones stay: crypts above fens above kiln, the Vault at the bottom. On some nights the glass happens to reflect the city the same way for everyone who walks it. Hespfoot calls these **Common Nights**: the daily seeds. Replaying a seed is "walking the same reflection again".

**What the Meta-Ledger is.** Chained to a lectern at the Stair-Head is the **Wardens' Ledger**, written in lamp-black scraped from Ysolde's lantern. Soot from a flame outside the hour can't be turned back, so it is the only paper in Senwick that remembers. It records each descent: how deep, how fast, which relics were carried, how many times a partner was relit, which phantasm pockets were found. Its back pages are the **Roll of the Restless**, the bestiary. Lantern Trims are noted in its margins.

**What descending means.** Each floor down is closer to the kiln where the Stillglass was poured, and to the moment the hour was sealed. The deeper you go, the thinner the reflection and the more it resembles the real night: hotter, faster, less forgiving.

## 5. Biomes and Floors

The three floors are stacked in the order Senwick was built. The Mirror-Palace is different: it is the network of light-halls that ran through every level, so its wings can turn up on any floor.

### Floor 1 — The Sunken Crypt

**Mood:** cold, wet, patient. Dripping, and water moving somewhere you can't see.

**What happened:** On the last night the Synod opened the river sluices to cool the kiln for the pour. The water ran down through the ossuaries, and since that night has never ended, it has never stopped. The crypts stand in streams that rise and fall to the same mark every night.

**Hooks:**
- Every niche has a grave-lamp. Most are dry; a few still hold oil, because the Wardens tending them were mid-round when the hour was sealed.
- Tide-lines on every wall at one height, worn deep as a chisel cut.
- Echo-Crystals in the burial niches chime when something passes; it is how the crypt warns its dead. Crypt Crabs live in the skulls. Puddle Newts sleep in the footprints of things that walk the same path every night.
- Tally marks scratched by someone who counted the nights and stopped at four hundred and twelve.

### Floor 2 — The Blighted Spore Fens

**Mood:** humid, soft, crowded with small lights. Fog thick enough to lose your partner in.

**What happened:** These were Senwick's farms: mushroom beds, eel ponds, gardens lit by mirror-shafts. With no tomorrow, no season ever ended. The fungus never finished fruiting, so it fruited on itself, until the fens became one slow rot. The Runners' poison is distilled from it.

**Hooks:**
- Prism-Lilies were bred to open under mirror-light. The shafts are dark now, so they open only for the Bearer's lantern, and a trail of blooms closes slowly behind you.
- Lumen Moths and Lantern Beetles crowd any light, the Bearer's included. Pulse-Moss beats in time with nothing.
- A harvest calendar with the same date scratched in over and over. Baskets of fish on a dock, perfectly fresh, every night.
- The old shaft-mouths, black and blind, with pale bleached patches on the stone where daylight used to land.

### Floor 3 — The Cinder Kiln

**Mood:** hot, loud, red. Cracks in the floor glow like lit fuses.

**What happened:** A mirror that holds an hour must be kept at the heat it was poured at, or the hour will set and crack. The Synod gave that job to the kiln crew and their master, the Kiln-Mother, and sealed them in with it.

**Hooks:**
- Huge round moulds, cracked, with the shape of the Stillglass cooled into the floor.
- Pay-chits for the crew, all dated the same night, all unpaid.
- Painted over the crew's door, many times by different hands: "KEEP HER WARM."
- Oil barrels stacked carelessly by the vents. The crew stopped worrying about fire a long time ago.

### Throughout — The Obsidian Mirror-Palace

**Mood:** cold, bright when lit and black when not, very quiet. Your footsteps come back a little wrong.

**What happened:** These are the Synod's light-halls, the galleries that carried daylight down into every street. When the Stillglass caught the city, they became the back of the mirror, where Senwick's reflection is cast each night and where it is thinnest.

**Phantasm walls and pockets:** In places the reflection has worn so thin that a wall is only a picture of a wall. A careful lantern shows them: they don't throw your light back the way real glass does. Behind them are **phantasm pockets**, small rooms that are not reflected at all. They are pieces of the real Senwick as it was on the last night, which is why the reshuffle flows around them.

**Hooks:**
- Mirrors that show rooms you haven't entered yet.
- Your reflection moving a half-beat late. The Wardens' rule: a late reflection is honest; a prompt one is not.
- In the pockets: dust (nothing else in Senwick has dust, because dust needs time), a cold cup of tea, and the shards and pages described in section 10.

## 6. Enemies and the Boss

Senwick's dead have died without finishing many thousands of times. What is left of each is the job they were doing when the hour was sealed.

**Tappers (Exploders).** Senwick's miners tapped at the rock and listened for collapse. They died in the deep seams with lungs full of firedamp, and the gas has been building ever since. Slow, swollen, and drawn to noise, because noise used to mean danger and their bodies still go to check. Up close, the gas finds a spark. A broken pot or a chiming crystal will pull them across a room, which the Bearer can use.

**Runners (Throwers).** The Runners carried flasks between the glassworks: kiln-oil, fen-rot for etching, silvering solutions. They were fast in life and are faster now. In a held hour, time is the only thing worth stealing, and the Runners have learned how. Their worst flasks hold **stillsmoke**, the vapour of Stillglass dust. Breathe it and your moments are drawn out long, and the Runner drinks the difference: every bit of speed you lose, they gain. They are thieves of seconds, and nervous about it, the way thieves are.

**Slips (Mirror Stalkers).** When someone died in front of a mirror on the last night, sometimes the reflection stayed behind. A reflection needs light to exist, so in darkness a Slip is nothing, and the moment your lantern touches it, it is there. Slips walk through phantasm walls because they are made of the same thing. Dark Vision shows their outline before the light does, which the Wardens considered the only safe way to meet one.

**The Cinder Colossus.** The Kiln-Mother, **Hettie Brand**, was told to keep the Stillglass warm, and she did. When the fuel ran low she fed the kiln whatever was to hand, then the crew's tools, then, over the centuries, the crew and herself. What stands before the Vault now is the kiln walking: slag, firebrick and slumped shapes around a glowing heart, with one instruction left. In its second phase it sounds the old kiln whistle, and the Tappers in the walls come to it as stokers, to be burned. Killing it lets the Vault cool. Its heart is a bead of the last sunset Senwick ever saw, cooked into glass.

## 7. NPCs

### Tamsin Vell, Keeper of the Brazier

Tamsin was a Wickwarden on watch at a brazier when the Stillglass was poured, and she has not let it go out since. Because she has kept a flame tended the whole time, the hour never quite closed on her. She remembers, and she is tired. She cannot leave the brazier's circle.

Every Sanctuary brazier in Senwick was lit from one fire, so they are all the same fire, and Tamsin is in all of them. At each Resting Ground the Bearer finds her shape sitting in the coals. She is dry, practical and kind, and does not believe the Bearer can fix anything until about the tenth time they come back. She keeps a copy of the Ledger and teaches the Wardens' craft. She knew Ysolde, and does not like talking about her.

### Nibbot, the Glass Pedlar

Senwick's last travelling merchant was a man named Tobbin. He died in front of a shop mirror and his reflection stayed. Most Slips go feral; this one kept Tobbin's habits instead, and has been setting up his stall for three hundred years for customers who mostly try to eat him.

He speaks backwards, because reflections do. The gibberish voice is reversed speech, and his subtitles spell each word backwards in the right order. He introduces himself as "Nibbot", so that is what everyone calls him. He wants gold because Tobbin wanted gold; he has no idea what he'd do with it. He likes the Bearer, the first customer in a long time who pays. Late on, he lets slip that he remembers the lantern.

### The Third Eye

The sense altar at each Sanctuary is a standing stone carved with a single eye, far older than Senwick. Before the city there were people in the Hesp who never used fire, the **Dimfolk**, and the stone is theirs. The Wardens learned their dark-craft from it: press gold into its cracks as an offering, sit with it, and the body remembers senses it had before people had lamps.

The Ledger names its three gifts **the Dimfolk's Eye** (Dark Vision), **the Long Ear** (Acute Hearing, sound seen as rings in the dark) and **the Warm Trail** (Scent and Thermal trails). The eye on the front of the stone is open. On the back, which almost nobody looks at, the same eye is carved closed.

### Ysolde Crane, the Warden in the Glass

The real Ysolde walked out with the lantern, lived a long life in Hespfoot, started the chapel custom, and is buried in its yard. But her reflection stayed in the Stillglass, and from there it can reach the phantasm pockets. She is the one leaving shards and pages there. She is seen only through plain glass, in unreflected places, and she looks like someone who has been waiting a long time to apologise.

## 8. Combat Kit Lore

The Bearer starts with a lamplighter's habits. The rest is Warden craft taught by Tamsin, tricks learned from the things trying to kill you, and tools found in the dark. **Lantern Arts** are permanent once learned (recorded in the Ledger). **Kept Tools** fill the two skill slots and are found during a run.

## 10. Story Beats and Endings

The story is carried by Tamsin's talk, the Ledger, environment details and the pages in phantasm pockets. Each beat is triggered by progress the Ledger has recorded; nothing is forced.

### Within a run

1. **Stair-Head at dusk.** The Ledger lies open. On the first descent it holds one line, in Ysolde's hand: "Keep it lit."
2. **Sunken Crypt.** First Tappers and Runners. First Sanctuary: Tamsin, the brazier, the Third Eye. First stall: Nibbot.
3. **Spore Fens.** Fog thickens, Runners get bolder, and the fens make clear that time is stuck, not just that the city is dead.
4. **Cinder Kiln.** "KEEP HER WARM." The Colossus.
5. **The Vault.** Inside stands the Stillglass, a tall round mirror showing Senwick on its last night, lit, busy and alive. The Bearer breaks it.

### Across many runs

- **First deaths.** Tamsin explains the loop, the soot, and why the Bearer remembers. Others have come down from Hespfoot over the centuries. None lasted.
- **Early Sanctuaries.** Tamsin tells the official history: army, Synod, Stillglass. The army comes up so often that the player should notice it no longer matters.
- **First co-op descent.** Tamsin, quietly pleased: "Two wicks. She'd have liked that."
- **First phantasm pocket.** Dust, a cold cup, and a shard of **plainglass**: unsilvered glass that reflects nothing and shows only what is actually there. Beside it, a page in Ysolde's hand. The Ledger opens a new section, **Pages from Behind the Glass**.
- **Ysolde's pages** (one per new pocket, around twelve in all) tell the other version of the last night. The Stillglass needed an anchor: one moment kept outside the hour, holding it shut from the far side like someone leaning on a door. The Synod chose a flame. Ysolde's walk out of the city was not an escape. It was the last step of the pour.
- **Mid-game.** Nibbot remembers the lantern. Tamsin admits she knew. The closed eye on the back of the Third Eye is found.
- **First Vault cleared.** Ending one, below. The next dusk, the glass has grown back.
- **Deeper nights.** Each break sets the reflection back nearer to the real moment of the pour. After ending one, the Vault floor shows a stair further down, and every descent past it is a deeper, harder night, with its depth recorded in the Ledger.
- **The last page** says what the shards are for, and what the closed eye means: "Dark was never what we were afraid of. We were afraid of morning."

### Ending one — "A Little Earlier"

The Bearer breaks the Stillglass. Light floods the Vault, the reflected city shatters, and for a moment everything is still. In Hespfoot the sun rises a few minutes earlier than last year, and the bell-ringer stops ringing. Credits.

Then the Ledger turns a page: dusk at the Stair-Head. Tamsin, at the next brazier: "It grows back. It always grows back. But that was a good morning." It is a real ending, and an incomplete one. Only the reflection was broken; the anchor still burns.

### Ending two — "The Shuttered Lamp" (true ending)

With every plainglass shard, the Bearer assembles a **Plain Pane** at the Stair-Head. Held up to the Stillglass, it shows what the glass is reflecting: a door behind it, into the one room in Senwick that was never reflected. This is the **Unreflected Vault**, where the hour was sealed.

The Synod stands around a table, mid-sentence, exactly as they were. On the wall is an empty Warden's lamp-hook, and beside it, seen through the plain glass, Ysolde.

The truth is simple. The hour is held because the flame still burns outside it. Hespfoot's lamplighters kept the chapel lantern lit faithfully for three hundred years, and every year of that faithful tending cost the valley a few more minutes of morning. The Bearer's own lantern has been holding the night shut.

The Bearer hangs the lantern on the hook and closes the shutter. In co-op, both bearers close theirs together. The screen goes fully dark and stays dark long enough to be uncomfortable. Sounds: the Synod finishing their sentence, chairs scraping, a long breath going out of the whole city. Then a thin grey line, then sunlight, real sunlight, coming down the old shafts for the first time in three hundred years and running mirror to mirror through every street, the way they were built to carry it.

The dead lie down and finish. Tamsin's brazier goes out, and she lets it. The Bearer walks out of the Hesp with a cold lantern into a morning that arrives on time.

The last line in the Ledger, in the Bearer's hand, in the last of the soot: "Put it out. Went home."

## 11. In-Game Text Samples

### Title-screen tagline

> Some nights are kept on purpose.

### Opening narration (first descent)

> In Hespfoot the dawn bell is rung by hand, at the hour the sun should rise. This year the ringer rang it in the dark, and kept ringing, and nobody came out to tell him to stop.
>
> Under the hill is a city that kept one night for itself, three hundred years ago. It has been drinking our mornings ever since.
>
> The chapel lantern has never gone out. It came out of that hill with the last of the Wardens, and every lamplighter since has kept it.
>
> Now it's yours. Take it down the Stair. Whatever is holding the night is holding it in the dark, and you have the only light down there that isn't theirs.

### Vendor lines (Nibbot)

Each word is spelled backwards in normal word order. Suggestion: subtitles show only the reversed text until the Bearer has the second tier of the Long Ear, after which the meaning appears beneath.

1. "Olleh, olleh! Nibbot si nepo." [Hello, hello! Tobbin is open.]
2. "Dlog rof lio, lio rof dlog." [Gold for oil, oil for gold.]
3. "On sdnufer. Eht ruoh sevig gnihton kcab." [No refunds. The hour gives nothing back.]
4. "Ruoy pmal si dlo. I rebmemer ti." [Your lamp is old. I remember it.]
5. "Esolc eht rettuhs dna netsil." [Close the shutter and listen.]

### Loading-screen tips (from the Ledger's margins)

1. Tappers come to noise. Break a pot on purpose, then walk the other way.
2. If a Runner's smoke has your legs, it has your seconds too. Get out of the green before you swing.
3. A wall that doesn't throw your light back isn't a wall. Hold the lantern up to it and look again.
4. Oil is heavier than gold and worth more. Tamsin will tell you so, at length.
5. Two wicks lit from one flame can relight each other. Don't wander off with the only match.

### Run-death message

> The wick gutters. The hour turns back, and you with it. Dusk again at the Stair-Head.

### Victory message (ending one)

> The glass is broken. Somewhere above, a little earlier than last year, the sun is coming up.

### Victory message (true ending)

> The lamp is out. The morning came on time.
